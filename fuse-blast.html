Formlabs: Fuse Blast

I owned the media hopper for the Formlabs Fuse Blast from early prototype to production: the subsystem that filters powder and worn-out media out of the blast media, so it stays clean and lasts longer.

The Problem

Fuse Blast cleans SLS-printed parts by blasting them with media that recirculates continuously. Every pass picks up loose powder from the parts, and the media itself slowly breaks down. In most blasters the media degrades fast: even a little powder in it means you're blasting parts with powder instead of media, and they never get fully clean.

Filtering the Media

Media fires through the blast nozzle into the cabinet. Air handling carries it back out and drops it onto a mesh, and from there it returns to the hopper, cleaned on every lap.

  • 01
    A 45° mesh as a tangential flow filter: Gravity slides the media along the mesh. Anything smaller than the holes, including loose powder and media worn too small to blast effectively, falls through into a waste chamber. Keeping both out of circulation extends media lifetime. (See Fig 1.2)
  • 02
    Tuning the angle: The mesh had to be steep enough that material doesn't build up on it, but shallow enough that media stays on it long enough to filter. I worked out that 45° does both.
  • 03
    Extruded tracks: The mesh rides in custom extruded aluminum tracks, so it can slide out for maintenance. (See Fig 1.3)
Fig 1.2: The 45° filter mesh, pulled out for maintenance.
Fig 1.3: Extruded aluminum tracks that hold the 45° mesh.

Feeding the Nozzle

  • 01
    Metering valve: Suction from the blast nozzle pulls media through a sand-cast metering valve that sets the feed rate. The valve lets air in to mix with the media, so it flows as a dilute stream instead of a dense slug that would move slowly and clog. (See Fig 1.4)
  • 02
    Built-in knife gate: A knife gate built into the same casting shuts off the flow so the hopper can be emptied for maintenance. (See Fig 1.5)
Fig 1.4: Sand-cast metering valve with a built-in knife gate.
Fig 1.5: The knife gate in action.

Serviceability & Production

  • 01
    Lit windows: Rather than add level sensors, I designed lit windows so users can see the media and waste levels directly. Powder coats everything inside the hopper, so finding window and light positions that stay readable took real work.
  • 02
    Doors that line up with the shells: The hopper isn't attached to the machine's cosmetic shells, but its doors mount to the hopper and still have to line up with them. Fitting doors that work with both was one of the trickier packaging problems.
  • 03
    Valves and interlocks: I designed the valve touchpoints users operate to empty the hopper, and added the hardware interlocks.
  • 04
    Sealing: I checked that the hopper was sealed with pressure decay testing: pressurize it, then time how fast the pressure drops.
  • 05
    Built for cost: Fuse Blast is a cost-sensitive machine, so the hopper is built from bent sheet metal, extruded aluminum, and sand-cast parts.
