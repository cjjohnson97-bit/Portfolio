The Engineering Challenge

The Formlabs Fuse Blast was engineered to automate the labor-intensive cleaning cycle for selective laser sintering (SLS) 3D printed parts. The system combines abrasive blasting media, pressurized air jets, and automated basket rotation to depowder complex nylon geometries in minutes.

From a mechanical perspective, the unit presented aggressive challenges: containing abrasive blasting media, preventing powder leakage with tight seal interfaces, maintaining high structural rigidity under cyclic pneumatic loads, and scaling to cost-effective global mass production.

Hopper Subsystem & Fluidized Powder Handling

I owned the complete mechanical development of the abrasive media hopper subsystem. Key engineering accomplishments included:

  • 01
    Gravity-Fed Funnel Geometry: Optimized wall angles and internal transition fillets to eliminate media bridging and rat-holing across varying humidity conditions.
  • 02
    Fluidized Media Metering: Integrated positive-seal metering valves and pneumatic aeration ports ensuring smooth, continuous media delivery into the blast gun without clogging.
  • 03
    Abrasive Wear Protection: Selected hardened wear plates and specialized elastomeric gaskets at high-impingement zones to guarantee a multi-year service life under continuous blast cycles.

Sheet Metal Architecture & Robotic Seam Welding

To achieve high structural stiffness while reducing unit manufacturing cost, the main chassis was designed around heavy-gauge sheet metal weldments:

  • 01
    Robotic Seam Welding Fixtures: Designed tab-and-slot self-aligning features throughout the weldments to eliminate complex assembly jigs and enable robotic torch path repeatability.
  • 02
    Warp Mitigation & Thermal Distortion Control: Performed FEA thermal stress simulations to balance weld heat inputs, preventing flange warping across critical gasket sealing surfaces.
  • 03
    Hermetic Pressure Sealing: Achieved continuous IP-rated particulate containment preventing abrasive glass bead and nylon dust egress into external environments.

Die-Casting DFM & Global Supply Chain Sourcing

To transition high-stress structural and fluid components from machined prototypes to mass production, I led extensive Design for Manufacturability (DFM) iterations with international casting vendors:

  • 01
    High-Pressure Aluminum Die-Casting: Engineered uniform 3.0mm nominal wall thicknesses, generous draft angles (1.5°–3.0°), and optimized parting lines to maximize die tool longevity.
  • 02
    Mold Flow & Porosity Control: Collaborated with foundry toolmakers on runner/gate locations and overflow wells to eliminate gas porosity near critical post-machined O-ring grooves.
  • 03
    Vendor Management & First Article Inspection (FAI): Managed international suppliers across Asia and North America, reviewing CMM dimensional reports, GD&T datum schemes, and material test certifications.
