What It Runs

A Raspberry Pi at home runs my DNS ad blocking (Pi-hole), remote access (Tailscale), and weekly backups. It's also where I build small projects. I describe what I want and AI tools write the code, which has turned into some of the most fun engineering I do.

  • 01
    A smart scale without the app: My scale only shared my weight through an app that kept logging me out and wouldn't export a CSV. It turns out the scale broadcasts each reading over Bluetooth Low Energy, no pairing needed. An ESP32 in my room listens for those broadcasts, filters them, and gets the readings onto the Pi, where they're mine. It also hosts its own web page, so I can update its firmware over Wi-Fi instead of USB.
  • 02
    Lights that know when the TV is on: My own home automation runs the usual schedules, like lights on at sunset, without sending my data to a cloud service. It also does something off-the-shelf systems can't: the living room lamp glares on the TV, so the Pi, which is also my DNS server, looks up the TV's address and pings it. TV on, lamp off; TV off, lamp available again. A lamp that used to sit unused now gets used.
  • 03
    Over-the-air TV, anywhere: An antenna and tuner record free broadcast TV, which I can stream from anywhere. I built it as a backup in case my streaming service didn't work while I was abroad, and it recorded a season finale we watched from overseas.
