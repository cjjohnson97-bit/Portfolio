EP4739424A1 — Liquid Level Sensing Apparatus & Method

View on Google Patents ↗ Download Patent PDF 📄

Abstract & Scope: An innovative, non-intrusive fluid monitoring apparatus and method utilizing localized pressure differential gradients to accurately measure volatile liquid levels, eliminating sensor fouling, liquid sloshing errors, and surface meniscus distortion.

The Rescue Mission

During late-stage validation, the original liquid level sensing mechanism suffered severe unreliability: sensor drift from chemical vapor deposition, false dry-run readings from foaming/bubbles, and erratic meniscus adhesion that threatened to stall product launch.

I was brought in to lead an emergency turnaround of the subsystem. Through rigorous root-cause analysis and DOE (Design of Experiments) testing, I identified that optical and capacitive sensors were fundamentally incapable of withstanding the volatile chemical environment and fluid viscosity shifts without frequent recalibration.

First-Principles Redesign: Pressure Gradient Tech

Instead of relying on fragile optical interfaces, I conceived and engineered a novel fluidic pressure gradient architecture:

  • 01
    Differential Micro-Pneumatic Sampling: Replaced submerged electronics with an isolated pneumatic sensing port that interrogates hydrostatic head pressure via an engineered micro-bubble gradient.
  • 02
    Immunity to Sloshing & Vapor Condensation: The pressure gradient methodology isolates all active electronic transducers behind an inert gas buffer, rendering the measurement 100% immune to vapor condensation, fluid coloration, and viscosity variations.
  • 03
    Sub-Millimeter Level Resolution: Designed a tuned orifice and damping chamber that filters out dynamic pump pulsations, achieving reliable ±0.5mm fluid height accuracy across full operating temperatures.

Results, Impact & Patent Prosecution

The turnaround was completed ahead of the revised tooling freeze. Rapid CNC prototypes and endurance test rigs validated over 100,000 cycles with zero sensor failures or drift.

Due to the novel physics and commercial value of the mechanism, Formlabs IP counsel filed for international patent protection, resulting in the granted European Patent EP4739424A1.
