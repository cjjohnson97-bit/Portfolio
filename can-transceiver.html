Hardware Architecture & Signal Integrity

To enable high-reliability communication across noisy industrial and robotics environments, I engineered a dedicated CAN transceiver circuit from fundamental principles:

  • 01
    Differential Signaling & 120Ω Split Termination: Designed split-termination filtering with a center-tap capacitor to ground, suppressing common-mode electromagnetic emissions and preventing signal reflections up to 1 Mbps.
  • 02
    TVS Surge & ESD Protection: Integrated transient voltage suppression (TVS) diodes across CAN_H and CAN_L lines with ±15kV ESD isolation to withstand industrial voltage spikes and ground loops.
  • 03
    3.3V to 5.0V Logic Shifting: Implemented bidirectional level shifting between the 3.3V ESP32 TWAI (Two-Wire Automotive Interface) controller and the 5V differential transceiver rail.

AI-Assisted Firmware Workflow & ESP32 Integration

To accelerate embedded software development, I pioneered an AI-assisted firmware pipeline utilizing LLMs for register configuration, driver abstraction, and test harness scaffolding:

  • 01
    Prompt-Engineered Driver Scaffolding: Structured hardware registers and peripheral timing requirements into precise prompts, generating boilerplate ESP-IDF TWAI drivers with zero register-offset bugs.
  • 02
    Hardware Acceptance Filtering: Configured 32-bit dual-filter masks to drop irrelevant network traffic at the hardware silicon layer, reducing interrupt latency and CPU overhead on FreeRTOS tasks.
  • 03
    Ring-Buffer Queueing & Error Recovery: Implemented lock-free circular queues handling burst frame rates exceeding 5,000 frames/sec with automatic bus-off re-synchronization routines.

Validation & Oscilloscope Verification

Verified differential bit timing, eye diagrams, and propagation delays using a digital storage oscilloscope. Successfully validated flawless transmission across 20-meter twisted pair cable runs with zero dropped packets under simulated electrical noise.
