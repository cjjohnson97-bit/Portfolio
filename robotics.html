My Path in FIRST

  • 2012
    Team 3958, Schrodinger's Cat (founder, captain): I founded the team at Boston College High School and captained it for its first three seasons.
  • 2018
    Team 4362, Gems (mentor): This is where I learned to get it done today. The lead mentor put me in charge of a subsystem, and whenever I wanted to order parts and build tomorrow, he pushed back: what scrap do we have, what can we weld, how do we get it on the robot now?
  • 2020
    Team 4909, Bionics (lead mechanical & design mentor): This is where I learned to be a mentor instead of the designer. Students design the robot; my job is to point our CAD students at mechanisms that will work and help them not fail.

How I Mentor

FIRST is a teaching program, so students design the robot. My job is to make sure what they design wins: I'm responsible for the robot's mechanical performance, and I steer each student as much as they need.

  • 01
    Design reviews: I run reviews from a slide deck of screenshots of the students' CAD, marking up what to change and why.
  • 02
    Teaching the why: If an intake needs more stiffness, I walk the student through why. If a motor won't fit, I'll suggest a clever place to package it. If a belt is skipping teeth, I'll suggest a way to design that failure mode out.
  • 03
    Two steps ahead: I keep track of how every mechanism will work and go together, so students don't design themselves into a corner.
  • 04
    Getting it done today: I teach students the shop, from drills and lathes to our CNC router, mill, and lathe, and how to keep a build moving. Instead of waiting on a McMaster order, I ask the students: what can we change about the design to finish it today?
  • 05
    Points on the board: We run the season like an agile team, ranking priorities by what scores more points right now and working the top one. A mechanism doesn't count until it's on the robot and programmed.
